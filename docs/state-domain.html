(A) 2024 Measures by State and Domain

How each state performs within each Core Set domain in 2024. A state result is on track (scored better than the median) when its rate is at least as good as the CMS median for that rate. This is direction-aware: for lower-is-better rates, such as hospital admissions or low birth weight, a rate below the median number is the better result and counts as on track. A rate exactly equal to the median counts as on track. Counts are of measure rates: each rate definition counts separately, e.g. 7-day and 30-day follow-up. The two depression-screening rates (CDF) are left out because their 2024 state rates are not publicly reported. Data: data.medicaid.gov, Quality theme.

Overview — top 10 and bottom 10 states

Score = measure rates the state reported and was on track on (at or better than the median) ÷ all measure rates the state reported in the selection. To keep a state that reported only one or two rates from topping the list on a small denominator, a state must report at least half of the selection's rates to be ranked (). Ties are broken by number of rates reported. Values shown: score · on-track rates / reported rates.

      Domain composite — how the nation is doing by domain

      For each program × domain: how many states reported, how many distinct measures (CMS abbreviations) and measure rates the domain holds, and the % of reporting states mostly on track — states where more than half of their reported rates in that domain are at or better than the CMS median (exactly half does not count).

      How to read the %: the CMS median splits reporting states roughly in half on every single measure, so this composite cannot show absolute national improvement. What it shows is breadth. Above 50% means on-track results are spread across many states (most states are on track on most of the domain's rates). Below 50% means they are concentrated in a smaller group of states while most states are not on track on most rates. For change over time, see Trends 2022–24.
