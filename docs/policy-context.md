# Policy Context — why these measures matter, and what is changing

**Last reviewed: 2026-11-12.** This page is maintained by hand and sits *outside* the annual
data-refresh cycle, because legislative status changes faster than the Core Set data does.
It is **forward-looking context, not a causal explanation** of any 2022–2024 measure movement:
almost every event below postdates the data in this build. Election results cited here were
**unofficial and uncertified** at the time of review.

## 1. Why Core Set reporting matters

### Reporting significance

The Child and Adult Core Sets are the only standardized, cross-state view of the quality of
care delivered through Medicaid and CHIP, which together cover roughly 80 million people. They
are how CMS, states, and the public compare states on prevention, behavioral health, maternal
health, chronic-condition care, and patient experience. CMS publishes the results each year on
[data.medicaid.gov](https://data.medicaid.gov/datasets?theme%5B0%5D=Quality) — the data this site
is built from.

### Requirements

- The **Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018** (Child Core Set) and the **SUPPORT Act of 2018**
  (behavioral-health measures in the Adult Core Set) made annual reporting **mandatory starting
  with FFY 2024**.
- CMS codified the requirement in the
  [mandatory Core Set reporting final rule (88 FR 60278, Aug. 31, 2023)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-08-31/html/2023-18669.htm),
  at [42 CFR Part 437](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/437.1). It applies to the 50
  states, DC, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands and Guam.
- States must use CMS's measure specifications. Rates computed with other specifications are
  flagged and left out of the CMS median.
- **Stratification** is phased in: beginning with 2025 reporting, states stratify a subset of
  mandatory measures by race and ethnicity, sex, and geography, and the subset grows each year
  ([CMS stratification resource](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/quality-of-care/downloads/QMR-stratification-resource.pdf)).
- The non-behavioral-health Adult Core Set measures remain **voluntary**. That is why Adult
  domains such as Long-Term Services and Supports show many fewer reporting states on the
  [(A) State and Domain](state-domain.html) page.

### Cadence

- **Annual.** CMS issues a State Health Official (SHO) letter each year with that year's
  measure list. For example,
  [SHO #25-005](https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/sho25005.pdf) sets
  the 2026 Core Sets. The 2026 Child Core Set adds Oral Evaluation During Pregnancy
  (OEVP-CH).
- States submit through CMS's Quality Measure Reporting (QMR) system. **2026 Core Set reporting
  is due December 31, 2026** ([2026 QMR webinar](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/quality-of-care/downloads/2026-QMR-Webinar.pdf)).
- Measures change through an annual stakeholder review run by Mathematica for CMS. See the
  [2028 Core Set review final report](https://mathematica.org/-/media/internet/features/2026/child-and-adult-core-set/2028coresetreview-finalreport.pdf).
- The data lag: a "2024 Core Set" mostly reflects services delivered in calendar year 2023.

### Connection to reimbursement and value-based care

Core Set results are not paid on directly. They do, however, feed into most of the
places where Medicaid ties money to quality:

- **Managed-care quality strategy and state-directed payments (SDPs).** Under the
  [2024 managed care rule](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/cms-finalizes-new-standards-and-reporting-obligations-affecting-medicaid-and-chip-managed-care-plans),
  SDPs must advance the goals of the state's managed-care quality strategy. States usually
  evaluate those goals with Core Set / HEDIS measures.
- **Medicaid & CHIP Managed Care Quality Rating System (MAC QRS).** States must publish
  plan-level ratings on a mandatory measure set that overlaps heavily with the Core Sets,
  starting with measurement year 2026 and displayed by the end of 2028.
- **Plan contracts.** Quality withholds, performance incentive pools, and auto-assignment
  algorithms in state managed-care contracts usually key off the same HEDIS-based measures
  (well-child visits, follow-up after hospitalization, prenatal/postpartum care, and others).
- **Section 1115 and value-based purchasing (VBP) arrangements.** Waiver evaluations and
  provider VBP models commonly use Core Set measures as their outcome or incentive metrics.
- **CMS "Universal Foundation."** CMS's effort to align a core set of quality measures across
  Medicare, Medicaid and the Marketplace draws heavily on Core Set measures.

## 2. Current policy and funding landscape (as of November 2026)

### The 2025 reconciliation law (H.R. 1, P.L. 119-21, the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act")

The single most consequential input. Based on CBO's score as allocated by KFF, it reduces federal
Medicaid spending by about **$911 billion over ten years**
([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/health-provisions-in-the-2025-federal-budget-reconciliation-law/))
and raises the number of uninsured by about 10 million by 2034. The three largest Medicaid
levers ([Morgan Lewis, Nov. 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/2026-midterm-postmortem-medicaid-cuts-and-work-requirements)):

- **Work / community-engagement requirements** (~$326B). Expansion adults aged 19–64 must show
  80 hours a month of work, school or service. CMS issued an
  [interim final rule (CMS-2454-IFC)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/03/2026-11094/medicaid-program-community-engagement-requirement-certain-individuals)
  on June 3, 2026 ([CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicaid-community-engagement-requirement-certain-individuals-interim-final-rule-comment-period-cms)).
  States must implement **by January 1, 2027**; some states started earlier.
- **Provider-tax restrictions** (~$191B). Limits on the taxes most states use to fund their
  share of Medicaid.
- **State-directed payment limits** (~$149B). New SDPs are capped at 100% of Medicare rates in
  expansion states and 110% in non-expansion states. Grandfathered SDPs phase down 10
  percentage points a year from 2028. A
  [May 2026 CMS proposed rule](https://cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicaid-managed-care-state-directed-payments-medicaid-fee-service-targeted-medicaid-practitioner)
  would go further ([Georgetown CCF analysis](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/05/28/cms-triples-harmful-impact-of-hr-1-medicaid-provider-cuts-in-state-directed-payment-proposed-rule/)).
- The law also created the **$50B Rural Health Transformation Program** (2026–2030). First-year
  awards to all 50 states were announced on Dec. 29, 2025
  ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/cms-announces-50-billion-in-awards-to-strengthen-rural-health-in-all-50-states.html)).
- **Litigation.** A federal judge denied a preliminary injunction sought by 26 state
  attorneys general on Oct. 29, 2026. Four states (OR, MN, NM, WA) filed a new suit on Nov. 5,
  2026 ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/2026-midterm-postmortem-medicaid-cuts-and-work-requirements)).

### Marketplace subsidies

The enhanced ACA premium tax credits **expired at the end of 2025**. The House passed a
three-year extension on Jan. 8, 2026, but it has not become law
([KFF FAQ](https://www.kff.org/faqs/faqs-health-insurance-marketplace-and-the-aca/what-kind-of-recent-changes-to-the-marketplace-premium-subsidies-have-happened-that-i-should-know-about/)).
This affects the population at the Medicaid / Marketplace boundary, which is the same population
that churns in and out of Medicaid.

  still moving and uncertified at the time of review

  [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/2026_ballot_measures)).

## 3. How these changes may show up — in quality measures vs. other metrics

Core Set rates are **rates among the people enrolled**. They do not count the people who lose
coverage. A policy that shrinks enrollment can therefore leave a Core Set rate flat, or even
improve it, while access gets worse overall. Read the two kinds of signal side by side:

| Policy change | Likely signal in Core Set quality measures | Better captured by other metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Work requirements / 6-month redeterminations (2027+) | Fewer people meet continuous-enrollment rules, so denominators shrink and become healthier. Possible drops in well-care, prenatal, follow-up-after-ED and SUD-treatment measures for adults who churn | Enrollment and disenrollment counts, procedural-termination rates, uninsured rate (ACS), churn / re-enrollment within 90 days |
| Provider-tax and SDP limits (2028+) | Lagged. Narrower networks, so access-sensitive measures (dental, behavioral-health follow-up, postpartum) may fall 1–3 reporting cycles later | Provider participation, MCO network adequacy, hospital margins, appointment wait times |
| State administrative strain (eligibility systems) | Fewer voluntary Adult measures reported. More "other specifications" flags. Thinner reporting depth on page (A) | Count of measures reported per state, timeliness of QMR submissions |
| Rural Health Transformation awards | Possible gains in rural-stratified rates (geography stratification) | Program progress reports, rural facility closures and openings |
| Expansion in TX / GA / WI (if enacted) | Adult Core Set denominators grow with newly covered, often sicker adults. Rates may *dip* at first even as access improves | Coverage gains, uncompensated-care costs |
| ACA subsidy expiration | Indirect. Shifts in who sits at the Medicaid eligibility boundary | Marketplace enrollment, premiums, uninsured rate |

**Implication for this site:** the 2024 data predates all of the above. Treat the
[(A)](state-domain.html) and [(B)](measures.html) pages as the **baseline**. When 2026–2028
Core Set data arrive, compare state rates *and* reporting depth against this baseline, and pair
any change in a rate with enrollment data before reading it as a quality change.

## 4. Retained from the previous Policy Context panel

- **Reporting-regime change inside the trend window.** The 2024 Core Set made Child Core Set
  reporting, and the Adult behavioral-health measures, **mandatory**. This is a
  data-comparability event within 2022–2024; see the [Trends page](trends.html) comparability
  note.
- **States respond unevenly.** States fund about a third of Medicaid on average (~35% in FFY
  2024), so a federal reduction has an amplified effect on total program spending. Some states
  are absorbing costs from general funds; others are more exposed to the provider-tax and
  expansion-financing changes.
